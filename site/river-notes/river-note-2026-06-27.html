The rate eased for a second week; Marseilles is still the binding lock

The system average is down about 2% to $13.46 a ton, corn export demand holds firm, and the forward read is withheld as too stale to publish.

Ken Beegle · published June 27, 2026 · figures as of June 23, 2026
Topics: Rates, River conditions, Grain

Rate pressure is easing at the margin. The system-average modeled rate slipped to $13.46/ton (from $13.76, about 2% below the prior week) and the average pressure index fell 40.2 → 36.1 — an elevated-but-cooling market, not a break.

The Illinois system stays the tightest corridor, Marseilles is the binding chokepoint, and corn is the demand engine. The forward read is withheld this week: the forward leg's newest snapshot is 25 days old, past the freshness line.

Marseilles is the lock that binds

Lock friction is concentrated at Marseilles on the Illinois River. The most recent reading is June 26, 2026: 14 tows and a 13.2 hour average wait — the queue building even as the per-tow wait came down from 18.4 hours on June 21. Twenty of the twenty reviewed lock-days in the window are flagged as constraints.

The 10-day forecast points the low-water watch at the three upper-basin gauges with the lowest forward flows: USGS gauge 05331000 at 10.4 thousand cubic feet per second and a 3.5 foot stage, 05420500 at 52.2 and 9.3 feet, and 06893000 at 62.7 and 13.7 feet.
