# Golden writing examples

These pairs demonstrate the rule being tested. They are examples, not templates to copy when the
evidence differs.

## Use the default report voice: factual persuasion

**Avoid:** Kirby's Q2 can reveal where scarce capacity earns a premium.

**Use:** Kirby's July 29 report can show where scarce equipment earns more — and where high
utilization fails to lift rates.

The revision makes a narrower promise, supplies a date, names the economic tension, and gives the
reader a reason to continue. Before selecting a report title, draft at least 10 alternatives
privately and choose the most specific version the evidence can support.

## Give the reason why and the decision

**Avoid:** Utilization is not a universal pricing signal.

**Use:** Genesis reported 95.9% inland utilization and lower day rates in Q1 2026. Full equipment
therefore did not guarantee pricing power. Compare renewal spreads by product and corridor before
changing a rate card. Source: Genesis Energy Q1 2026 filing, May 7, 2026.

The section moves from supported fact to interpretation to reader action. A material counterexample
or disproof condition belongs next when one exists.

## Lead with the supported finding

**Avoid:** This report examines recent changes in barge rates and river conditions.

**Use:** Lower Mississippi barge rates fell $0.39/ton last week as the St. Louis gauge rose 1.8 ft.

The second sentence must name the source and as-of date before publication.

## Distinguish fact from inference

**Avoid:** Higher permit awards prove industrial demand is recovering.

**Use:** Awarded permit value rose 18% from a year earlier through June 30, 2026 (USACE permit
records). That is a leading signal of possible construction demand, not observed barge tonnage.

## State an inconclusive result

**Avoid:** Mixed signals suggest the market remains resilient.

**Use:** The evidence does not distinguish between stable demand and a delayed reporting cycle:
contract value increased, while the latest observed tonnage remains one quarter old.

## Explain an estimate in reader language

**Avoid:** The nowcast remains robust with 0.72 in-sample correlation across 16,860 rows.

**Use:** On weeks the model did not see during training, its barge-rate estimate lands within about
$5.27/ton of the observed rate, widening to about $9.44/ton during drought. Source: model evaluation
run `<run ID>`, as of `<date>`.

## Finding-led chart title

**Avoid:** Figure 3: Barge rates, 2020–2026

**Use:** Figure 3: Barge rates now exceed the 2022 peak

## Rate table header and values

**Avoid:** `Location | Rate | WoW` followed by `20.94 | ↓ 0.39`.

**Use:** `Location | Rate ($/ton) | Change vs. last week` followed by `$20.94 | ↓ $0.39`.

## Commit subject

**Avoid:** Update files for retry handling.

**Use:** Retry fax delivery after a Telnyx 5xx response.

## Pull-request opening

**Avoid:** This PR makes several changes to the report generator.

**Use:** Block report publication when a required figure lacks an as-of date. The generator now
validates figure metadata before rendering; tests cover missing, malformed, and valid dates.
